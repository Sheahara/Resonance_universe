# CASCADE - OPENING
## July 6, 2026 | San Rafael & Bay Area

---

### I. THE CALL

The morning had been unremarkable in that specific way Ava had learned to treasure. Sophie was showing Mira something on her tablet—a game, maybe, or one of those videos where people restored old things. The sound of her daughter's laughter mixed with the smell of the French toast Marcus was attempting in the kitchen, and for a moment Ava let herself believe this was just a Sunday.

Then her phone rang with Collin's ringtone, and she knew.

She'd assigned him a specific tone after June. After everything. Not because she expected him to call—he hadn't, not once since Sciara withdrew from the complaints—but because she needed to be ready if he did. Needed that half-second of preparation before she answered.

"Collin." She was already moving toward the back patio, away from Sophie's bright voice. Mira's eyes tracked her, reading her posture the way she'd learned to over years of friendship.

"Ava." His voice was wrong. Too controlled. Collin Blake was a trauma surgeon; he knew how to stay calm when everything was falling apart. That was the tell. When he sounded *this* calm, things were very bad. "I need—Sciara's—"

He stopped. She heard him breathe.

"Tell me," she said.

"Vertigo. She can't stand without falling. The headache hasn't broken in three days—no, that's not right, it's been building for a week but three days since she could function through it. And Ava, I think—" Another breath. "I think she's been seizing. I didn't see it happen but she's post-ictal. I'm almost certain she's post-ictal."

Ava closed her eyes. "The lupus?"

"Completely out of control. I talked to her rheum on Friday and adjusted the prednisone but it's not touching it. Nothing's touching any of it."

"The app," Ava said, though she already knew. She'd been watching Sciara's readings remotely through a backdoor she wasn't supposed to have. Watching the numbers deteriorate and feeling helpless. "What does it show?"

"Shield integrity under five percent." Collin's voice cracked on the number, just slightly. Just enough for her to hear the husband underneath the surgeon. "Ava, I'm not taking her back there. I don't care what I have to do. If you can't help me, I'll take her to Zuckerberg or Stanford General, I'll find a civilian hospital that won't ask questions, but I am not putting her back in that building."

"You won't have to."

"What?"

"I said you won't have to." Ava turned and saw Mira standing in the doorway, tablet forgotten, Sophie's game playing to no one. "Collin, listen to me. I have the authority to authorize an inter-agency transfer if it's medically necessary. Even if I didn't, you're her proxy. You're requesting it. The ICR can't refuse a proxy's demand for transfer of care."

"Sevron will—"

"Sevron can file whatever objections she wants after the fact. By then your wife will be at Falling River and it won't matter."

She heard him breathing. Processing. "You can do that?"

"I can do that." She was watching Mira's face, watching understanding bloom there. "I'm going to call ahead and have them send a private ambulance. FRMC has their own vehicles. That way we don't trigger anything on emergency channels—ICR monitors those. You won't have to explain anything to anyone who might report back."

"How long?"

"Forty-five minutes to an hour, depending on traffic. I'm leaving now too. Mira and I will meet you at your place."

Mira's eyebrows rose slightly—*I'm coming?*—but she didn't argue. They'd known each other too long for that.

"Mira," Collin said, and there was something complicated in his voice. Gratitude and guilt tangled together. He knew Mira was the easy target. He knew what kind of risk she was taking just by being present.

"She knows," Ava said. "We're on our way."

She ended the call before either of them could talk themselves out of it.

---

### II. THE KITCHEN CONFERENCE

Marcus looked up from his French toast when Ava came back inside. He had the particular expression of a husband who'd been married long enough to read emergencies in the set of his wife's shoulders.

"Go," he said, before she could speak. "Sophie and I will be fine."

"I don't know when I'll be back."

"I know." He was already reaching for his phone, probably to cancel whatever plans they'd had for the afternoon. "The Blake thing?"

She nodded. Marcus didn't know everything—couldn't know everything—but he knew enough. He knew his wife came home some nights with a particular kind of silence that meant she'd seen something she couldn't fix. He knew about the complaints filed and withdrawn, about the meetings that had ended with Ava staring at the wall for an hour afterward.

"Be careful," he said.

"Always."

Mira was waiting in the hallway, already pulling on her jacket. "I need to get a few things from my car."

"Mira." Ava caught her arm. "You don't have to come. You understand what this might—"

"I understand." Mira's voice was quiet but firm. "I was there, Ava. March thirty-first. I was the one who called for medical after the containment breach. I *watched* Sevron fit that collar herself, before anyone else arrived. I've been carrying that for three months and I couldn't do anything with it because what was I going to say? Who was I going to tell?"

She'd never actually said it out loud before. Ava had suspected—had pieced it together from the way Mira flinched whenever Sciara's case came up—but this was the first time.

"She placed it herself?"

"She was already in the room when I got there. Said she'd been passing by and heard the alarm. Said she stabilized the situation. But Ava, she had the collar *with* her. Already calibrated. Already keyed to Sciara's resonance signature." Mira's jaw tightened. "You don't just have those lying around. You don't just *happen* to have one ready."

Ava felt something cold settle in her stomach. "You're saying it was planned. The breach, all of it."

"I'm saying I don't know what I'm saying. I'm saying I need to see this through."

"Sevron will know you were there. When this plays out, she'll know you were involved."

"Then she'll know." Mira met her eyes. "You tried to give me deniability. I know you did. But I would have wanted to participate anyway, and you knew that too, which is why you let me come over this weekend in the first place."

Ava couldn't argue with that. She'd invited Mira specifically because she'd felt something building. Because she'd wanted her close in case—

In case of exactly this.

"I've put you in a bad spot," Ava admitted.

"You've put me in the spot I'd have put myself in." Mira's smile was grim. "Let's go save someone."

---

### III. THE AMBULANCE

Aria was already in the bay when the call came through, because Aria was always in the bay lately. Even six weeks post-delivery, she couldn't seem to stay away from the hospital. Alexander said it was because the twins slept better in her office than at home; Aria said it was because she'd lost too much ground during the mandatory leave.

The truth was somewhere in between, and neither of them talked about it.

"Blake residence, San Rafael," the dispatcher said. "Kessler authorization, emergency inter-agency. Patient is Sciara Blake."

Aria's hand stopped halfway to her coffee cup.

"Say again?"

"Sciara Blake. Kessler is invoking emergency transfer. Private transport requested to avoid ICR notification."

The coffee was forgotten. Aria was already moving, pulling her coat off the hook, mentally cataloguing what she knew. Shield failure. Probably the collar, finally doing what she'd feared it was doing all along. She'd seen the readings Ava sent her in the encrypted channel. She'd watched the deterioration and felt helpless.

"I'm riding," she said.

"Dr. Shahrazai—"

"I'm riding." She was already out the door, heading for the ambulance bay. "Get Alexander. Tell him—no, I'll tell him. Just get the rig ready."

Alexander found her in the ambulance thirty seconds before they pulled out. He didn't ask questions, just climbed in beside her and started checking supplies. They'd been married long enough for that kind of shorthand.

"It's Sciara," she said, as the vehicle started moving.

"I gathered." His hands kept moving—IV kits, monitoring equipment, the specialized resonance-dampening gear that FRMC ambulances carried. "How bad?"

"I don't know yet. Bad enough for Collin to call Ava. Bad enough for Ava to invoke emergency transfer without going through any other channels."

"Shield integrity?"

"Under five percent."

Alexander's hands paused for just a moment. Then he kept working. "The collar?"

"That's what I need to see." Aria pulled up the portable diagnostics unit, running through the setup. "Ava's theory is it's been running an adaptive protocol that's gone completely off the rails. The readings she's been getting—the device is interpreting normal stress responses as volitional resonance use and suppressing accordingly."

"That's not possible with a Series VI."

"No. It's not."

Alexander met her eyes. They didn't need to say the rest. If the collar wasn't a standard Series VI—if it was something else, something modified, something that should have been pulled during the recall—

"Sevron," he said.

"Probably."

"This is going to get complicated."

"I know."

He reached over and squeezed her hand, just once. "Okay. Let's go see what we're dealing with."

---

### IV. THE HOUSE

Collin heard the car pull up and felt something unknot in his chest, just slightly. Just enough to move.

Sciara was on the couch where he'd positioned her, propped against cushions in a way that minimized the vertigo. She hadn't moved in twenty minutes. Hadn't spoken in longer than that. Her eyes were open but unfocused, tracking something he couldn't see.

The collar hummed against her throat. It had been humming louder lately—a sound he wasn't sure she could even hear anymore, but he could. A vibration just below the threshold of audibility that set his teeth on edge.

"Hey." He crouched beside her, taking her hand. "Help is coming. Ava's here."

Sciara's eyes moved toward him. He watched her try to process the words, watched the delay between hearing and understanding. It was getting worse. Every day it was getting worse.

"Ava," she repeated. The word came out slurred, like she was speaking through water.

"Yes. And some doctors from Falling River. They're going to help you."

"No." Her hand tightened on his, suddenly strong. "No, Collin, I can't—she said—"

"Shh." He brought her hand to his lips, pressed a kiss against her knuckles. Grounding. Anchoring. The thing they did that nobody else saw. "You're not going back to ICR. I promise. This is different."

"She'll hurt—"

"Nobody is going to hurt anyone. I've got you."

The doorbell rang. Sciara flinched, her whole body tensing, and the collar's hum shifted pitch. Collin watched the indicator light on its surface flicker from amber to red.

"It's okay," he said, even as his stomach dropped. "It's just Ava. I'm going to let her in and then I'm coming right back. Thirty seconds. Can you count to thirty for me?"

Old language. Their language. The thing that worked when nothing else did.

Sciara's breathing slowed. "One," she whispered. "Two."

He made himself let go of her hand. Made himself walk to the door. Made himself unlock it and step aside to let Ava and Mira in.

"She's in the living room," he said. "The collar keeps—it's reacting to everything. Every time she gets scared, it—"

"I know," Ava said. Her face was carefully neutral but her eyes weren't. "Let me see her."

They walked in together, and Collin heard Mira's sharp intake of breath.

Three months. It had only been three months since the last time Mira had seen Sciara—briefly, awkwardly, at an ICR function neither of them had wanted to attend. Three months shouldn't make this much difference. Three months shouldn't turn a vibrant, brilliant woman into this hollow-eyed figure struggling to count past fifteen.

"Oh," Mira said softly. "Oh, Sciara."

Sciara's eyes found her. Something moved across her face—recognition, confusion, fear—and the collar's hum spiked again. The light went from red to flashing red.

"It's doing it again," Collin said. "Every time she has any kind of emotional response, it interprets it as—"

"Volitional resonance use," Ava finished. Her voice was flat. Professional. Hiding something Collin couldn't name. "The adaptive learning protocol. It's not supposed to be able to do that."

"What do you mean, not supposed to?"

"I mean Series VI collars don't have adaptive learning." Ava was kneeling beside Sciara now, looking at the device without touching it. "They have static parameters. They suppress according to pre-set thresholds. They don't *learn*."

"Then what—"

"I don't know yet. But I'm going to find out."

The ambulance pulled up outside, lights off, no sirens. Collin saw it through the window and felt Sciara tense beside him.

"That's the doctors," he said. "From Falling River. They're friends, Sciara. Aria's coming."

"Aria." Sciara's voice caught on the name. "Aria knows. Aria... the pattern is..."

She stopped. Her face crumpled with frustration, words escaping her.

"I know," Collin said. "You don't have to explain. Just let them help."

The door opened. Aria came through first, Alexander behind her, both of them carrying equipment Collin didn't recognize. Aria's eyes went straight to Sciara and stayed there.

"Oh, honey," she said softly. "Oh, sweetheart, look at you."

The collar shrieked.

It wasn't a sound—not exactly—but everyone in the room felt it. A resonance spike, a suppression response, the device interpreting Sciara's relief at seeing a friend as a threat to be neutralized. Sciara gasped and her hand flew to her throat, clawing at the metal.

"Don't touch it," Aria said sharply. "Collin, hold her hands. Alexander, I need the scanner."

Collin grabbed Sciara's wrists, gentle but firm. Holding her still. The thing they practiced. The trust they'd built. "I've got you," he said. "Look at me. Just look at me. They're going to help but you have to let them."

Sciara's eyes locked onto his. Wild. Terrified. But she stopped fighting.

Alexander handed Aria a device Collin didn't recognize—sleek, white, humming with its own subtle frequency. Aria ran it along the collar's surface, watching the readout.

"That's not a Series VI," she said after a moment. Her voice had gone very quiet. "The housing is VI but the internals—the behavioral signature—this is a VII Adaptive. It's running a learning protocol I've never seen before."

"VII was recalled in April," Ava said. "Every unit was supposed to be replaced."

"Well, this one wasn't." Aria looked up at Collin. "How long has she been like this? The escalation pattern?"

"It's been building for weeks. Months. But the last ten days—"

"Something triggered a cascade." Aria was scanning again, her frown deepening. "The device is interpreting baseline physiological stress as volitional resonance activation. Fear response. Pain response. It's been punishing her for being *afraid*."

"That's not—" Collin's voice broke. "That's not possible. Sevron said the collar was helping. She said—"

"Sevron lied." Mira's voice was flat. Hard. "Sevron's been lying since March."

Everyone looked at her.

"I was there," Mira said. "When they put the collar on. Sevron already had it. Already calibrated. She was waiting."

Silence. The collar hummed. Sciara whimpered.

"We need to move," Aria said finally. "We can't remove this here—it's too integrated, and if the failsafes are as corrupted as the rest of it, removal without proper support could—" She stopped. Started again. "We need to get her to Falling River. Now."

"Can you sedate her for transport?" Collin asked. "So the collar doesn't—"

"No." Aria shook her head. "Sedation reads as a resonance event. It would trigger a suppression spike. We have to keep her conscious and as calm as possible."

Collin looked down at his wife. At her terrified eyes and the metal thing wrapped around her throat. At the flashing red light that meant the device was fighting her own nervous system.

"I'll ride with her," he said.

"Collin—"

"I'll ride with her. She'll stay calmer with me there. You know she will."

Aria hesitated. Then nodded. "Alexander, help me get her on the stretcher. Ava, you're following in your car?"

"Right behind you."

They moved, all of them, with the kind of efficiency that came from crisis. Sciara let herself be lifted, positioned, strapped down. Her eyes never left Collin's face.

"Stay with me," she whispered. The words were slurred but clear enough.

"Always," he said. "That's the deal. Remember?"

She almost smiled. Almost.

The collar's light flickered amber for just a moment—the closest to calm it had been in days—and then they were moving.

---

### V. THE AMBULANCE (REPRISE)

The first spike came before they'd cleared the neighborhood.

Aria was monitoring the collar's output, watching the numbers climb and fall with each of Sciara's heartbeats. They were bad numbers. Worse than she'd expected. The suppression field was eating through what remained of Sciara's shields like acid through paper.

"EVE baseline is at nineteen percent of expected," she told Alexander, keeping her voice low enough that Sciara might not hear. "Shield integrity is functionally zero. The device has been running at ninety-plus percent suppression for—" She checked the logs. "God. For *weeks*."

"Can anything sustain that?"

"No." Aria's jaw tightened. "Nothing can sustain that. That's the point."

In the back of the ambulance, Collin was holding Sciara's hand and talking to her quietly. Aria couldn't hear the words—didn't need to. The tone was enough. Low, steady, intimate. The voice of someone who knew exactly how to reach another person in the dark.

She'd wondered about them, sometimes. About the way Sciara always seemed to settle when Collin was close. About the particular quality of trust between them. She'd never asked. It wasn't her business, and anyway, she thought she understood.

The collar shrieked again.

Sciara arched off the stretcher, gasping, and the readings spiked into the red. Aria grabbed for the dampening kit even as Alexander was already moving.

"What triggered it?" Collin demanded. "She was calm, she was—"

"The device is interpreting the ambulance movement as a threat response." Aria was working fast, hands steady despite the fear twisting in her gut. "The vibration, the motion—it's reading her vestibular disorientation as resonance preparation."

"That doesn't make any sense."

"No, it doesn't. Unless—" She stopped. Looked at the collar again. At the readings that didn't match any protocol she knew. "Unless someone programmed it to be this sensitive on purpose."

"Why would anyone—"

"To break her." The words came out flat. Clinical. The only way Aria could say them. "To create a no-win scenario. If she's calm, the baseline suppression degrades her shields. If she's afraid, the adaptive response punishes her for the fear. If she *tries* to do anything—use her abilities, fight back, even just *think* about resistance—the device escalates. There's no winning move."

Collin's face went pale. "You're saying this was designed to—"

"I'm saying I need to examine the device properly before I say anything definitive. I'm saying I've seen this pattern before, in documents I wasn't supposed to read. I'm saying—"

The collar shrieked a third time, louder, and Sciara screamed.

Not a sound. A *resonance*. The kind of uncontrolled burst that happened when someone's abilities overloaded their capacity to contain them. It shattered the rear window of the ambulance and set off car alarms for a block in every direction.

And then Sciara went limp.

"She's seizing," Alexander said. "Aria—"

"I see it." Aria was already moving, positioning Sciara, protecting her airway, doing the things her training demanded while her heart pounded in her throat. "Collin, I need you to keep talking to her. I don't care if she can hear you, I need you to keep talking."

"Sciara." His voice was raw. Broken. "Sciara, stay with me. Come on, love. Come back. You're stronger than this. You're the strongest person I know."

The seizure lasted forty-three seconds. It felt like hours.

When it stopped, Sciara's eyes were open but empty. Staring at nothing. The collar's light had gone steady red—the highest suppression level—and it was no longer responding to the portable controls.

"It's locked us out," Aria said. "Director override required."

"Then get Sevron on the phone and—"

"No." Aria's voice was sharp. "Sevron is the last person we're calling. We're twenty minutes from FRMC. The resonance diagnostics team can override manually once we're there."

"And if she seizes again before then?"

Aria looked at Collin. At Sciara. At the device that was killing her friend centimeter by centimeter.

"Then we pray I remember enough from my surgical rotation to do a field removal." She took a breath. "But we're not going to let it come to that. Alexander, how fast can you get us there?"

"Hold on."

The ambulance accelerated.

---

### VI. ARRIVAL

Ava's car pulled into the FRMC ambulance bay thirty seconds after the transport. She'd broken several traffic laws getting there and didn't care. Mira was out of the passenger seat before the vehicle had fully stopped.

The ambulance doors were already open. Aria was calling orders—words Ava didn't fully understand, medical terminology mixed with resonance jargon—and a team was surrounding the stretcher.

Ava pushed through.

Sciara was unconscious. Or maybe not unconscious—her eyes were half-open, but there was nothing behind them. No recognition. No awareness. Just absence.

And then Ava looked at her aura.

She shouldn't have been able to see it. That wasn't her ability—she was administrative track, barely resonance-sensitive, not a reader or a healer or anything that would let her see someone's energy field. But Sciara's damage was so catastrophic, so profound, that it was visible to anyone who knew how to look.

Fissures. Cracks running through what should have been smooth and whole. Dark spots where the light should have been—suppression voids, she realized with horror. Places where the field had been pressed so hard for so long that it had simply ceased to exist.

"Mira," she said softly. "Are you seeing—"

"Yes." Mira's voice was barely a whisper. "Oh god, Ava. Her shields are gone. They're *gone*."

"Not gone." Aria was beside them suddenly, stripping off gloves smeared with something Ava didn't want to identify. "Shredded. There's a difference. Shredded we can work with. We can help her rebuild."

"Those voids—"

"Suppression voids. Long-term high-intensity field exposure." Aria's face was grim. "I've seen them before. They're rare. They're not supposed to happen with properly calibrated equipment."

"And improperly calibrated equipment?"

"Then they're exactly what I'd expect to see."

The stretcher was moving, the team wheeling Sciara toward the doors. Collin walked beside her, one hand on the rail, his lips still moving in that quiet constant stream of words.

"The collar," Ava said. "Did you figure out what it is?"

"VII Adaptive in VI housing. Running a learning protocol that's way outside normal parameters." Aria was walking with them, keeping pace with the stretcher. "It's been teaching itself to interpret her stress responses as resonance use. Every time she was afraid, it suppressed harder. Every time she felt anything, it punished her for feeling it."

"That's not how VII works. Even the prototype series—"

"No. It's not." Aria met her eyes. "Which means someone modified it. Someone with access to the source code and a very specific goal in mind."

"Sevron."

"I can't prove that yet. I need to get the collar off and run a full forensic analysis. But yes. Sevron."

They reached the doors. The team swept Sciara inside, toward the resonance diagnostics suite, and Ava caught a last glimpse of her friend—pale, still, the collar glowing steady red against her throat—before the doors closed.

Collin tried to follow. A nurse stopped him.

"Family waits in consult room three. Dr. Shahrazai will come to you as soon as—"

"I'm not waiting anywhere." His voice was ice. "That's my wife. I'm staying with her."

"Sir—"

"He comes with me," Aria said. "He's not civilian. Tag him as support personnel and let him in."

The nurse hesitated. Then nodded.

Collin followed Aria through the doors without looking back. Ava watched them go.

"What do we do now?" Mira asked.

"We wait." Ava pulled out her phone. "And I start making calls. By the end of today, Sevron's going to know what happened. I want to make sure the right people know first."

"What right people?"

Ava smiled grimly. "The ones who have been waiting for evidence that what she's doing isn't medicine."
